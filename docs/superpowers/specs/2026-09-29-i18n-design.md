## 0. The ask

> **Samuel, 2026-09-29:** *«vamos a trabajar en un working tree aparte para meterle i18n a todo
> vina modo seriedad (con inglés, español y alemán por ahora)»* — "let's work in a separate
> worktree to put i18n into all of Vina, serious mode (English, Spanish and German for now)".
>
> And, on seeing that Spanish was going to be the source: *«yo preferiría que todo el desarrollo
> se internacionalice (que todo pase a inglés como por defecto)»* — "I'd rather the whole
> development be internationalized (everything moves to English as the default)".

"Serious mode" is read as five commitments, confirmed in the conversation:

1. **Total coverage.** No surface mixes languages, in any of the three, the home's images and
   metadata included (§ 6.5). The sweeps of § 8.3 check the surfaces listed there and nothing else.
2. **A ratchet.** A new hard-coded string fails the pre-commit and, on a merge of `main`, the
   `pre-merge-commit` hook (§ 8.1), like the rest of the UI contract. The rule reads properties,
   module-level tables and toast objects, not only JSX (§ 8.1).
3. **Trade vocabulary comes from a glossary**, not from literal translation (pinza → dart /
   Abnäher, piquete → notch / Knipse, margen de costura → seam allowance / Nahtzugabe). All German
   in this spec is illustrative until the reviewer approves the glossary; for the notch, the
   sources the review found give `Knipse`, `Passzeichen` and `Kerbe` (§ 1.1 O7).
4. **María notices nothing** except changes decided on purpose: the decimal comma (§ 5.7) and the
   others accepted in § 1.1 O9, among them English strings that become Spanish (§ 7.3).
   She opens in Spanish whatever her browser's language is (§ 1.1 O5).
5. **The repository itself moves to English**: code, comments, tests, commits, specs, living docs.

"For now" means a fourth language must be cheap: one catalog folder, one glossary column, one
lock column.

## 3. Scope

**In: everything a person reads, or hears through a screen reader.**

- The app: chrome, panels, menus, command palette, dialogs, toasts, tooltips, `aria-label`s,
  empty states, errors, error boundaries.
- Product catalogs: fabrics, trims, 2D tools and their options, the UI names of body
  measurements, shortcut labels.
- The copilot towards the person: errors painted in the AI bar, its settings, suggestions, and the
  language it answers in.
- Documents for people: the BOM, whatever the 1:1 PDF prints, export toasts.
- The home: three static pages and what is baked into them: titles, descriptions, hero SVG labels,
  screenshots and OG images (the per-language assets of § 6.5).
- Static entry points: the app's tab title and `<html lang>` (`app/index.html:2,7`, § 5.4) and the
  manifest's `lang` (`public/manifest.webmanifest`, set to English; § 6.5).
- Numbers and key names.
- The repository's own language (§ 9.3).

**Out:**

- **Model-facing text:** tool descriptions (~232 tools, the code's own count in
  `src/copilot/action/tools/mensajeDeErrorEs.ts:15`), system prompts and tool results meant for the
  model stay English. The model is told which language to answer in (§ 6.3). Today 142
  description lines in 30 files also carry a Spanish half after a `/`
  (`src/copilot/action/tools/definitions/trimTools.ts:29-31`); phase 5 strips them in one mechanical
  pass (§ 6.3), because they cost prompt tokens and contradict "stay English". In the AI trace, a tool's
  error goes through `errorMessage` (§ 6.3); one it does not recognize appears as the tool wrote it,
  in English. That is the ceiling `mensajeDeErrorEs.ts:15` already declares.
- **Machine-read tokens:** DXF AAMA blocks and attributes (its only Spanish strings are comments),
  storage keys, IDs, `choices` values (`'flat-felled'`, `'outward'`), tool IDs in the AI trace (D6),
  and `src/utils/seamly2d/measurementNameTranslation.ts`. It maps the French, Russian and Ukrainian
  measurement names found inside third-party Seamly2D files to the canonical English keys the
  formula evaluator looks up (`formulaEvaluator.ts:12,63`). It is input parsing, not a UI
  mechanism: without it those references resolve to 0 (`formulaEvaluator.ts:68`) and the geometry
  degenerates. It stays as it is.
- **The person's data:** names she typed, and names generated when an object was created, are
  never re-translated when the language changes.
- Units (D11). CLI and server messages (five developer-facing files). Right-to-left scripts. Swiss
  German and British English.
- Blueprints as a catalog: there is no built-in library to translate. The slice starts empty and
  is dormant (`src/store/blueprintSlice.ts:4`) and its tools are unregistered
  (`src/copilot/action/tools/registry.ts:30`). Only the `…Es` fields of `src/types/blueprint.ts`
  go (§ 6.2).
- `languagePacks.ts`, `languageDetection.ts` and the "Idioma" selector of `AgentSettings`: deleted,
  not translated, with the edits their importers need (§ 6.3).
- Legal and privacy pages for a public launch in Germany: a product and legal question, not an
  i18n one, listed in § 1.1 O11 and not decided here. The one part that touches this design is the
  app's two Google-hosted fonts (`app/index.html:8-10`): self-host them (§ 1.1 O11).

## 4. Principles

1. **Translate at read, never at write.** What is stored is an ID, or a descriptor
   `{ code, params }` when the message has parameters; the text is asked of the catalog when
   painting or exporting. Precedents: #1194's `mensajeDeErrorEs`, and the trim dropdown that
   showed `nameEs` but stored `name`, so the BOM printed «Botón — Flat Button 15mm».
   - **Two exceptions are written down.**
     - **Transient text.** A toast lives 3 to 12 s
       (`src/store/copilotUISlice.ts:424`, `src/store/projectAutosave.ts:95`), so it may be
       translated when it is dispatched. It is not re-translated if the language changes while it is
       on screen.
     - **Derived, session-only data that each refresh replaces**: the BOM tab rows (§ 6.4). They are
       translated at refresh, `BOMState` records `extractedLocale`, and they go stale when the
       language differs. Manual rows are hers.
   - **Text that outlives a language change is a descriptor, formatted at paint.** Today it is
     stored as text: `Diagnostic.message` (`src/diagnostics/types.ts:116-121`, and
     `src/diagnostics/agentAnalyzer.ts:52` also takes the Spanish message as identity with
     `startsWith`) and `SeamResolutionError.message` (`src/store/inspectorSlice.ts:48-52`). Rules
     emit a `code`, and the agent analyzer switches on it. Each area's pass moves its own (§ 6.1).
     A chat message is a log: it keeps the language it was written in, like the model's replies.
   - **Reducers and `initialState` never call `t()`.** The caller resolves a default name and
     passes it in the payload (§ 6.2).
   - **A raw `error.message` never enters a translated sentence** (principle 2). It is a
     `{reason}` parameter or its own detail line.
2. **Whole sentences.** Never concatenate translated fragments: German reorders words, and Spanish
   and German agree in gender. Parameters go inside one message, and the message uses every
   parameter the English entry declares, a decimal number through `num()` and not `${x}`. `tsc`
   checks a catalog function's signature and its declared-but-unused parameters, not what the body
   prints (§ 5.2); the catalog parameter test of § 8.2 does. Text from elsewhere is a parameter or a
   detail line, never half of the sentence: `` `Error de construcción: ${error}` ``
   (`src/store/reactiveSolverMiddleware.ts:503`) and `` `Worker crashed: ${event.message}` ``
   (`src/workers/workerPool.ts:443`) are the shape to avoid.
3. **English is the source; the glossary governs all three languages.** Spanish that María signed
   moves verbatim into `es`, except number formatting (§ 5.7). It is not re-derived from English.
   A test compares each migrated `es` entry with the literal it replaced (§ 8.2).
4. **Variants:** `en` (US spelling), `es` (as today), `de` (Germany, with ß).
5. **Nothing visible in module-level constants.** Menus, shortcuts, tool definitions and presets
   are evaluated at import time; text stored there would freeze in the boot language. They hold an
   ID or a selector instead, one mechanism per module: an ID resolved in a `Record<Id, …>` of the
   catalog for data with an entry (§ 5.2), a selector `(t: Catalog) => string` for UI definitions
   such as menu items. Either way an item's identity is its `id`, never its visible text:
   `src/components/shell/LogoMenu.tsx:383-388` dispatches on `item.label === 'Importar DXF...'`
   today.
6. **What a machine reads is never translated.**

## 5. Core (`src/i18n/`)

### 5.1 Layout

```
src/i18n/
  locale.ts    Locale · SHIPPED_LOCALES · LANGUAGE_NAMES · getLocale · setLocale · subscribe ·
               detectLocale (§ 5.4). Imports only `src/utils/safeStorage.ts`
  en/          source: one file per area (shell.ts, editor2d.ts, viewport3d.ts, properties.ts,
               copilot.ts, exports.ts, errors.ts, keys.ts, catalogs.ts, …) and index.ts
  es/  de/     the same files; each area object `satisfies Catalog['<area>']`
  types.ts     Catalog = Widen<typeof en>   (imports `en` as a type only)
  format.ts    helpers(locale) → { num, plural } · formatNumber · formatCount · parseNumber
               (§ 5.7); formatNumber and formatCount read the active locale from locale.ts
  index.ts     catalogs: Record<Locale, Catalog> · t() · useT(); re-exports locale.ts and format.ts
  pseudo.ts    development-only pseudo-locale derived from `en`
  lock.json    the translation lock (§ 7.3)
```

Imports run one way: `locale.ts` ← `format.ts` ← the catalogs ← `index.ts`. Catalog files call
`helpers('en')` at module scope, and `formatNumber` needs the active locale. If `format.ts` took it
from `index.ts`, which imports the catalogs, the cycle would throw "Cannot access … before
initialization" depending on which file a test imports first.

### 5.2 Catalog shape

```ts
// src/i18n/en/pieces.ts: the source
const { num, plural } = helpers('en');
export const pieces = {
  delete: 'Delete piece',
  selected: (n: number) => plural(n, { one: '1 piece selected', other: `${n} pieces selected` }),
  lengthDiff: (mm: number) => `${num(mm, 1)} mm difference`,
};

// src/i18n/de/pieces.ts (German wording illustrative until the glossary review)
const { num, plural } = helpers('de');
export const pieces = {
  delete: 'Schnittteil löschen',
  selected: (n: number) => plural(n, { one: '1 Schnittteil ausgewählt', other: `${n} Schnittteile ausgewählt` }),
  lengthDiff: (mm: number) => `${num(mm, 1)} mm Differenz`,
} satisfies Catalog['pieces'];   // a missing key, or an extra one written in the literal, fails `tsc -b`
```

- `Catalog` is `Widen<typeof en>`: string literals widen to `string`, functions keep their
  signature, objects recurse.
- `satisfies` on an area object does not catch a missing area or an extra key that arrives through
  a spread. The registry is therefore typed `Record<Locale, Catalog>`, and a test compares the
  flattened keys of `es` and `de` with `en`'s (§ 8.2).
- `tsc` checks a catalog function's signature, not its body. `lengthDiff: () => 'Differenz'`
  compiles: a function with fewer parameters is assignable, and `noUnusedParameters`
  (`tsconfig.app.json:21`) only fires on a parameter that is declared and unused. So do `${mm}` in
  place of `num(mm, 1)`, which prints a period in German, and two parameters of the same type
  swapped. The catalog parameter test of § 8.2 covers them.
- Each catalog formats with **its own** locale's helpers, so German prints "12,3" even when the
  review document is generated while the app runs in English. `num(value, decimals)` is
  `formatNumber` for the catalog's locale: fixed decimals (§ 5.7).
- `plural` uses `Intl.PluralRules` of the catalog's locale. Spanish also has `many`
  (`select(1000000)` returns it), so `plural` takes
  `{ other: string } & Partial<Record<Intl.LDMLPluralRule, string>>` and looks up
  `forms[category] ?? forms.other`. `one` and `other` are enough for en, es and de; a language with
  more categories adds keys, not a mechanism.
- Data by ID (fabrics, trims, measurements, tools, shortcuts) are `Record<Id, …>` over an ID union,
  so an unknown ID in code is a type error. **Those unions do not exist today**: every ID is a
  `string` (`src/utils/cloth/physicalFabric.ts:51`, `src/types/trim.ts:29`,
  `src/tools/types.ts:27`, `src/shortcuts/ShortcutRegistry.ts:25`). Each domain gets an `*Ids.ts`
  module holding an `as const` tuple, with no import of `Catalog`; the data module types its `id`
  from it. Deriving the union from a data module that also holds `(t: Catalog) => …` selectors
  makes `Catalog` circular (TS2456). Phase 3 budgets the refactor: ~32 fabrics, 21 trims, ~60
  tools, ~70 shortcuts, and a `readonly` tuple ripples into `ShortcutRegistry`'s
  `ShortcutDef[]` parameter (`ShortcutRegistry.ts:44`). Tool options repeat IDs across tools
  (`mode` appears in five definition files), so they are keyed tool ID, then option ID.
- IDs read from a stored project stay `string`. `nameOf(catalog, id: string, fallback)`, with a type
  guard, is their one lookup (§ 6.2).

### 5.3 Access

- In React: `const t = useT()`, built on `useSyncExternalStore(subscribe, getLocale)`. A language
  change re-renders every component that calls it, without a reload. A component that paints
  catalog text or a formatted number therefore calls `useT()` itself, even when it needs nothing
  else from it: `formatNumber` reads the active locale but does not subscribe. Helpers that run
  during render take the catalog as an argument instead of calling `t()` on their own
  (`formatPieceLabel`, `src/utils/pieceLabel.ts`). The case to avoid is `SinglePiece`, a `memo`
  component (`src/components/PatternEditor/renderers/PieceRenderer.tsx:66`) that reads the fabric
  name in render (`:453`): with unchanged props it skips the re-render unless it subscribes itself.
- Outside React (middleware, exporters, copilot): `t()` returns the active catalog at call time. It
  does not subscribe, so it is for code that runs once per event, not for code that paints.
- Workers cannot call `t()`: they have no `localStorage` and their own copy of the module state.
  They and the solver report `{ code, params }`, as `SolverError` already carries a `code`
  (`src/utils/cloth/solverProtocol.ts:200-206`), and the main thread maps it through the catalog.
  Today the text travels as a string (`src/workers/pieceBuilderWorker.ts:94-95`,
  `src/workers/autoTraceWorker.ts:14-15`, `workerPool.ts:443`) and reaches a toast inside a sentence
  (`reactiveSolverMiddleware.ts:503`). A raw `error.message` is machine text: it follows a whole
  translated message, as a `{reason}` parameter or a detail line, and is never woven into a
  sentence (§ 4, principles 1 and 2).
- `t()` at module scope is forbidden (it would freeze). The lint rule of § 8.1 flags it, and flags
  the same for the helpers that read the catalog: `displayShortcut`, `displayModifierKey`,
  `formatNumber`, `formatCount`, `formatMedida`, `errorMessage`
  (`src/components/PatternEditor/renderers/HintText.tsx:10` and
  `src/components/shell/InputSchemeSection.tsx:7-10` call `displayShortcut` at module scope
  today), and `t()` inside a hook initializer that never re-runs (`useMemo(…, [])`,
  `useState(() => …)`). Indirect evaluation (`const LABELS = build()`) is invisible to a static
  rule; the live language-switch e2e of § 8.3 is the backstop.

### 5.4 State, persistence, detection

- The active locale is module state with a subscription, **not Redux**: it is not part of the
  document, Ctrl+Z must not undo it, and code outside React reads it synchronously.
- It is persisted in the `localStorage` key `vina-locale` through `safeGetItem` / `safeSetItem`
  (`src/utils/safeStorage.ts`), with the inline justification CLAUDE.md requires for LS-only
  writes: it must be read synchronously before the first render (IndexedDB is async and would
  flash the wrong language). The home shares the origin (homepage spec D4) but stays at zero
  JavaScript, so it does not write the key: it hands its language to the app through `?lang=`.
- `detectLocale()` decides in this order, before the first render. Only supported locales count:
  `SHIPPED_LOCALES`, which is all three unless the German review is late (the fallback of
  § 1.1 O6).
  1. **`?lang=xx` on `/app/`** (§ 1.1 O4). The home's "Open Vina" links carry it. A
     supported value is persisted as `vina-locale` and the parameter is removed from the URL
     (`history.replaceState`), so a reload or a bookmark carries nothing stale; an unsupported
     value is only removed. It overrides a stored choice: the link comes from the page the person
     just read.
  2. **The stored `vina-locale`**, when it is supported. A corrupt value, or `pseudo`, is ignored.
  3. **First-boot rule** (§ 1.1 O5). There is no `vina-locale`, but a `vina`-prefixed
     `localStorage` key (`vina-…`, `vina_…`), or an `openclo` one from before the rename, exists:
     the install predates i18n, so `es` is persisted once. Today the app is Spanish whatever the
     browser says, and the repo does not record which language María's browser prefers; without
     this rule she could open her saved work in English on launch day. Only a new visitor falls
     through.
  4. **`navigator.languages`**: the first entry whose primary subtag is supported (`es-VE` → `es`,
     `de-AT` → `de`, `en-GB` → `en`).
  5. **`en`.**
- `SHIPPED_LOCALES` narrows only the launch build (phase 10). Development, e2e and the review build
  treat every catalog as supported, so `de` is reachable through `?lang=` and `vina-locale` before
  it ships.
- Every branch that decides persists its result, so rules 3 and 4 run once per browser profile.
  Otherwise a new visitor's first session would write `vina`-prefixed keys and her second visit
  would read as a pre-i18n install.
- The rule must run before `migrateFromOpenClo()`, which writes `vina-rename-migrated` on the first
  boot of every browser, new visitors included (`src/utils/renameMigration.ts:134`). `src/main.tsx`
  therefore calls `detectLocale()` first. Its only static import is the migration, so that the app
  graph does not evaluate before storage is renamed (`main.tsx:1-15`); `locale.ts` can be a second
  because it imports only `safeStorage.ts`, which imports nothing. The key scan uses
  `safeStorageLength()` and `safeKey()` (`safeStorage.ts:112,125`).
- `<html lang>` and `document.title` are set at boot from that result and on every change: screen
  readers, `hyphens: auto` (German compounds need it), and the tab, history and bookmarks. Nothing
  in `src/` sets `document.title` today, and `app/index.html` pairs `lang="en"` (`:2`) with a
  Spanish `<title>` (`:7`). The static title becomes English (D5) and the catalog holds the other
  two.
- `<html lang>` names the interface language, but every page also holds text in others: tool IDs in
  mono (D6), the copilot's replies in the language the person writes in (§ 6.3), her own data, and
  each language's name in the menu. Those elements say so (WCAG 3.1.2), or a German screen reader
  reads `createPiece` with German phonetics:
  - `lang="en" translate="no"` on tool IDs and other identifiers
    (`src/components/shell/UnifiedBarChat.tsx:310`);
  - `translate="no"` on her data and on the container of the model's replies, with `lang` when it
    is known;
  - `lang="en|es|de"` on each language item (§ 5.5);
  - `<meta name="google" content="notranslate">` in `app/index.html`: the app translates itself, and
    browser auto-translation rewrites the text nodes React manages.
- The pseudo-locale is a session override and never touches `vina-locale` (§ 5.9).
- Phase 0 ships `detectLocale()` with its tests: the precedence above, `es-VE` → `es`,
  `de-AT` → `de`, an unsupported language → `en`, a corrupt stored value, `?lang=` valid and
  invalid, and the first-boot rule, including that a new visitor's second visit keeps the language
  detected on the first (§ 8.2). An e2e under `en-US` with an existing `vina`-prefixed key opens in
  Spanish (§ 8.3).

  covers one hint that embeds a modifier name.
- Letter and digit bindings do not change: `e.key` and the CLO3D mapping are the same in every

  layout-neutral combination or removed, a product decision outside i18n; and a test fails on any
  default whose key is a single character other than a letter, a digit or Space, so every printed
  shortcut can be typed on the US, German and Spanish layouts.

### 5.7 Numbers

- `formatNumber(value, { decimals, trim = false })` is fixed by default, like `toFixed`. It wraps
  `Intl.NumberFormat(locale, { useGrouping: false, minimumFractionDigits: decimals,
  maximumFractionDigits: decimals, signDisplay: 'negative' })`, so 12 with one decimal is "12.0" in
  `en` and "12,0" in `es` and `de`. `trim: true` drops trailing zeros ("12"), the cotas' policy
  (`formatMedida`, `src/components/PatternEditor/cotas/parseMedida.ts:19-24`). Formatters are cached
  per (locale, min, max): building one per call costs about 20 µs against 0.1 µs for `toFixed`
  (Node 22). Spanish gains the decimal comma the cotas already use, and in numbers that is the only
  change in how one looks: padding zeros and count grouping stay (§ 1.1 O9). Outside the
  three differences below, `formatNumber(x, { decimals: n })` is `x.toFixed(n)` in `en` and the same
  with `,` for `.` in `es` and `de`; the parity table of § 8.2 pins both, and the three differences.
  - `Intl` rounds the decimal string, so 1.005 gives "1.01" where `toFixed(2)` gives "1.00", and
    1.45 gives "1.5" where `toFixed(1)` gives "1.4". `Intl`'s rounding is accepted.
  - A negative that rounds to zero prints as zero: -0.04 with one decimal is "0.0", where `toFixed`
    and a bare `Intl` formatter print "-0.0".
  - A non-finite value prints as `String(value)` ("NaN", "Infinity"), as `toFixed` does, not as
    "∞".
- `formatCount(n)` prints an integer with the locale's grouping: "29.338" in `es` and `de`,
  "29,338" in `en` (`es` groups from five digits, so 1200 stays "1200"). It keeps María's
  "29.338 partículas" ("29,338 particles") and is consistent with D8: grouping is unsafe only on a
  decimal printed with a period, and a count has no decimals and is never parsed back. It replaces
  the hard-coded `toLocaleString('es-ES')` and `toLocaleString('en-US')` calls of § 2. The
  `toLocaleTimeString()` of § 2 (`src/components/BottomPanel/GradingTab.tsx:413`) takes the
  browser's locale; it moves to a time formatter that takes the active one.
  `src/copilot/memory/ProjectMemory.ts:217` builds model context and stays.
- The functions read the active locale when called. At module scope they would freeze the boot
  language, like `t()` (§ 5.3), so the check of § 8.1 flags them next to it; and a component that
  paints a number subscribes with `useT()`, or a language change leaves the old separator on
  screen. `format.ts` must not import `index.ts`: `index.ts` → catalogs → `format.ts` → `index.ts`
  is a cycle that throws "Cannot access 'helpers' before initialization" when a test enters
  through `format.ts`. The active locale lives in `locale.ts`, which imports only `safeStorage.ts`
  (§ 5.1, § 5.4);
  `helpers(locale)` gives a catalog the same functions bound to its own locale, and `num(x, d)` is
  `formatNumber(x, { decimals: d })` for that locale.
- `parseNumber(text, { suffix? }): number | null` is the single parser for every numeric text
  input. Its grammar is today's `parseMedida` (`parseMedida.ts:3`): an optional sign (ASCII or
  Unicode minus), digits, at most one `,` or `.` followed by digits, surrounding spaces, and the
  unit suffix the field itself paints, passed as `suffix` (`'mm'`, `'°'`, or a list). Anything else
  returns `null`, never `NaN` and never `0`: `1e3`, `+5`, `.5`, `12.` and "12 cm" in a millimeter
  field are `null`. A lone `,` or `.` is always the decimal separator, never grouping, because no
  Vina field prints grouping.
  - `"12,5"`, `"12.5"` and `"−12,5"` give 12.5, 12.5 and −12.5. `"1.250,50"` and `"1,250.50"`, with
    two separators, give `null`.
  - `"1.500"` and `"1,500"` give 1.5. A German who means fifteen hundred types `1500`. `parseMedida`
    and Chromium's own number field read `1.500` as 1.5 today, so the rule is old and only the
    audience is new. Where a thousandfold slip is plausible, the clamp of `NumericField` (below)
    catches it: roll widths are four-digit millimeter values (the presets reach 1500,
    `src/components/BottomPanel/NestingTab.tsx:24-28`), yet the custom roll width has `min={100}` as
    a bare attribute and its handler accepts 1.5 (`:146-151`, `:310-316`).
  - The policy on `null` belongs to the caller, and every caller is a field: it keeps its last valid
    value and marks the input (`aria-invalid`). Nothing dispatches 0, as `|| 0` does today
    (`src/components/BottomPanel/BOMTab.tsx:350,360`, `src/copilot/AgentSettings.tsx:275`).
  - A field seeds its draft with `formatNumber` and reads it back with `parseNumber`, and both
    sides move together: the seam-allowance field seeds with `String(...)` and reads with
    `parseFloat` (`ToolStrip2D.tsx:60,86`), and moving one side alone would read "1,5" as 1. In
    every locale `parseNumber(formatNumber(x, { decimals: d }))` returns `x` rounded to `d`
    (§ 8.2 tests it).
- **`NumericField`** replaces the 39 `<input type="number">` elements (`grep -rn 'type="number"'
  src --include=*.tsx` prints 40 lines; one is the comment at
  `src/components/PatternEditor/cotas/CotaChip.tsx:105`). In Chromium an `<input type="number">`
  sanitizes the text by the browser's own locale before JS sees it, so `parseNumber` cannot reach
  it as it stands (§ 2). `NumericField` is `type="text" inputMode="decimal"` with a string draft.
  It keeps ArrowUp and ArrowDown stepping by `step`, clamps to `min` and `max` on commit, parses
  with `parseNumber`, seeds and redisplays with `formatNumber`, and carries `role="spinbutton"`
  with `aria-valuemin`, `aria-valuemax` and `aria-valuenow`, so the `getByRole('spinbutton')`
  queries keep working
  (`src/tools/__tests__/donde-se-teclea-un-numero.test.tsx:92,125`). It is modeled on `CotaChip`,
  which dropped `type="number"` so that the wheel does not change the value and the decimal comma
  enters (`CotaChip.tsx:105-106,240-241`), and on `ToolOptionControls.NumberInput`. Fields lose
  the wheel nudge; that is one of the visible changes of § 1.1 O9. `type="range"` sliders and
  `<select>` never carry a separator and stay.
- **Phase 2 (§ 9.2) migrates every number in one pass over the whole repository**, so the comma
  changes once, in one known commit; phase 0 builds the functions and `NumericField`. A codemod on
  the census AST lists every candidate with its `file:line`, as § 6.1's does for strings:
  `toFixed(` and `toLocale*String` calls, `.replace('.', ',')`, numbers interpolated next to a unit
  (`${n} mm`), and each numeric input. It classifies each call, not each file:
  `src/utils/export/techPackPdf.ts:167` is machine and `:333`, a table cell, is not. The pass also
  rewrites the assertions that pin a period today.
  - What reaches a person moves. `toFixed` goes to `formatNumber` (`trim: true` for cota and notch
    copy), and the parsers of typed numbers go to `parseNumber`, through `NumericField` where the
    site is an input; `parseMedida` becomes a caller of it. `formatDimension`
    (`src/utils/units.ts:9-20`, "100.0 mm") stays fixed. `formatMedida` and `aTexto`
    (`CotaChip.tsx:28`), which hard-code the comma, become callers of `formatNumber`, and so do the
    hand-placed commas and raw interpolations of § 2. Parsers of files and formulas
    (`src/utils/dxf/parser.ts`, `src/utils/formats/svgImport.ts`,
    `src/utils/formula/safeEval.ts`) are machine input and keep `parseFloat`.
  - What builds machine output stays, and says so: it calls `toMachine(n, d)`, which is
    `n.toFixed(d)` under a name in `format.ts`. That covers DXF, SVG and OBJ coordinates

### 5.9 Pseudo-locale

`pseudo` exists only in development and e2e (`?locale=pseudo`) and never appears in the menu. It
is derived from `en` at runtime: accented letters, ~35 % expansion, brackets
(«[Ðéļéţé þîéçé ···]»). It makes any string that bypassed the catalog visible at a glance, and it
stresses layouts the way German does (§ 8.3).

- **Function entries** are opaque, so the derivation wraps them and transforms what they return.
  The wrapped functions still call `en`'s helpers, so pseudo does not exercise the decimal comma or
  the plural rules; `de` does.
- **Interpolated parameters:** before calling the `en` function, the wrapper puts each string
  argument (a piece name) between private-use sentinel characters, and the transform skips what
  sits between them and strips them afterwards. Data inside a message stays as typed, and the sweep
  (§ 8.3) tells data from catalog text. A number argument cannot be wrapped; it passes through and
  the transform leaves digits alone.
- **Persistence:** session-only. `?locale=pseudo` is read at boot and held in memory, never
  written to `vina-locale`, so a developer is never stuck in a language the menu cannot undo.
  `pseudo` is not in the supported set that `detectLocale()` validates a stored value against
  (§ 5.4), and `<html lang>` stays `en` while it is on.

### 7.3 The lock (`src/i18n/lock.json`)

For each key and each language (`en`, `es`, `de`), the lock stores the hash of the English source
entry at the moment that translation was written, the hash of the translation itself, and its
review: `reviewedBy` and `reviewedAt`. `en` is its own source, so it holds only its own hash and
its review: an edited English entry is unreviewed, and it goes stale only through the
signed-Spanish rule below.

**What the hash covers.** The entry's own tokens, read from the TypeScript AST so that
transpilation cannot change them: a string's value, or a function's parameter list and body.
Comments, whitespace and trailing commas are dropped and parameter names are replaced by their
position. Reformatting, a comment, or renaming `n` to `count` therefore does not stale three
languages; a changed word, or `${n}` where `num(n)` stood, does. The hash does not follow
references: an entry that interpolates a module constant would change meaning without moving its
hash. Entries reference only `num`, `plural` and their own parameters, and the lock test flags any
other module-level binding.

**What it can walk.** The test walks every property of every area object and asserts that the key
set it finds equals the flattened key set of `en` at runtime. A spread or a computed key that the
walker cannot read fails that test, so it cannot exempt an entry from the lock.

**Commands.** The CLI and the vitest lock test import one hashing module, the way
`src/utils/geometry/__tests__/pieceSpace.trinquete.test.ts:44` shares its ceilings with
`eslint-rules/deuda-espacio-de-pieza.js`.

- `npm run i18n:lock` reports, per area and language, the stale, untranslated and unreviewed
  entries, and writes nothing.
- `npm run i18n:lock -- --stamp <area|key>` records that a translation (`es` or `de`) was written
  against the current English. An area is accepted only for entries never stamped before. A stale
  key whose translation was rewritten is stamped plainly. A stale key whose translation has not
  changed since its last stamp is stamped only with `--reason "<why>"`: that acknowledges that
  English changed and the translations still hold. It takes one key at a time, and the reason
  stays in `lock.json`, so the diff shows it. There is no stamp-everything mode, because a blanket
  restamp would turn staleness detection off.
- The `reviewed` flag is written only by the review-apply step of § 7.5. Stamping never sets it.
  It holds only while the translation still hashes as stored: an edited translation is unreviewed
  again, and an acknowledgment leaves the flag alone.

**States.**

- **Stale** (the English changed and the hash no longer matches): the suite fails, always, and
  `.husky/pre-push` runs the suite, so a stale key blocks the push until it is written again and
  stamped, or acknowledged. This is what stops German from quietly saying the old thing. An edit
  to an English entry leaves its `en` unreviewed and makes its `es` and `de` stale.
- **Untranslated** (no entry, or an entry identical to the English text, unless it carries
  `// same-as-en: <reason>`, as a bare `DXF` label would): the suite fails, always. `satisfies`
  only guarantees that the key exists, and a stamp only records that something was written. The
  identity check is what stops English pasted into `de/`.
- **Unreviewed:** allowed while working. The launch merge requires zero for each published locale
  (`SHIPPED_LOCALES`, § 1.1 O6), with the named German reviewer as `reviewedBy` on every
  `de` entry (§ 8.4). After the launch, new strings enter unreviewed and reach the reviewer in
  batches.
- Spanish extracted verbatim from today's UI enters as reviewed: it is what María already signed,
  and the verbatim test of § 8.2 is its proof. The review-apply step records it once over the
  extraction batch, with `reviewedBy` set to `signed copy 473c1772`. English and German for these
  entries are written from her Spanish, which § 4, principle 3 keeps verbatim, so the lock also
  stores the hash of the Spanish as signed. When the `es` entry stops matching it, `en` and `de`
  for that key are stale until they are stamped again, which records the new hash. This is what a
  copy edit merged from `main` looks like when the resolver ports it into `es/` alone: no English
  hash moves. The merge procedure of § 9.1 lists the literals `main` changed or removed, and the
  resolver edits `en` first. Nothing re-translates a signed `es` entry automatically.
- Spanish written fresh (where today's UI still shows English) enters unreviewed until Samuel's
  pass (§ 1.1 O8); the `es` column of the new glossary terms is signed by María first
  (§ 7.1). English enters unreviewed until Samuel's pass; German, until the native pass.

**Order of review.** English is reviewed first and frozen area by area: an area is frozen when
every `en` entry in it is reviewed. The `es` and `de` review of an area starts after that, never
alongside its English, because each English correction stales that key's `es` and `de`, and the
German reviewer would read again what was already approved. After a freeze an English edit is
still possible, and it shows in the diff as stale keys.

### 7.4 Glossary test

If an English message uses a glossary term, the Spanish and German messages for the same key must
use theirs. The test enforces "one term per concept", and it makes a term change mechanical: when
the reviewer changes a term, it lists every string still using the old one.

- **Forms.** Each glossary row lists, per language, the forms to look for (`Naht · Nähte`,
  `margen · márgenes`, `Knopf · Knöpfe`). Both sides are folded before matching: NFD with the
  combining marks removed, `ß` as `ss`, lowercase. Without folding, umlaut plurals and accent
  shifts of core trade terms miss: `'nähte'.includes('naht')`, `'márgenes'.includes('margen')`
  and `'knöpfe'.includes('knopf')` are all false, and all true once folded. A form matches as a
  substring, so `Nahtzugaben` counts for `Nahtzugabe`.
- **Longest term first.** Text matched by a longer term is not offered to a shorter one: `seam`
  inside `seam allowance` is not a second term to find.
- **Function entries** are read from the AST, not called: the test takes the string and template
  parts of the English entry and of the translation, and ignores the expressions.
- **Exceptions** are declared inline, on the entry, with a reason:
  `// glossary-exempt: <term> — <reason>`. The test reads the comment from the AST.
- **Banned forms.** The same test reads the `banned` column (§ 7.2): an entry of that language
  that contains a banned form fails, unless exempted the same way. The glossary lists forms, not
  bare stems, because a stem such as `erb` sits inside `Verbindung`. Today one test pins the ban,
  and only for one panel's Spanish text
  (`src/components/PropertiesPanel/__tests__/EdgeVertexProperties.test.tsx:61`,
  `not.toMatch(/hered/i)`). This covers every entry in every language, once the tests move to
  catalog references (§ 8.2).

  calls every function entry in every locale with sentinel arguments and checks that each sentinel

### 9.1 Branch mechanics

- All work happens in `worktree-i18n`, with one merge to `main` at the end (D3). That merge is made
  with "Create a merge commit" only, never squash or rebase, and the PR description says so
  (§ 1.1 O2). Every sweep PR (§ 9.3) is merged the same way. A squash or a rebase gives
  commits new SHAs, and `.git-blame-ignore-revs` lists SHAs; a squash would also make ~1,000 files
  one commit nobody can review or bisect. Nothing in the repository fixes the style today: of the
  last 40 first-parent commits on `main`, 23 are merge commits and 17 are direct commits.
- **`main` into the branch.** A plain `git merge main`, never a rebase or a force-push. The
  orchestrator (§ 9.4) does it after each area closes and at least every 3 days. `main` moves fast:
  89 non-merge commits from 2026-09-22 to 2026-09-29, and 243 of the 800 non-test `.ts`/`.tsx` files
  under `src/` changed between 2026-08-31 (the start of the history the checkout holds) and
  `b0299b3b`. Fixes María is waiting for go to `main` first and reach the branch at the next merge.
- **The hook.** `.husky/pre-merge-commit` (§ 8.1) gates a clean merge, and a merge with conflicts
  concluded by `git commit` runs `pre-commit`. Growth in a file still on the debt list is a ceiling
  failure of the ratchet test, which runs at push: run `npm run test` after each merge, before
  pushing.
- **The audit.** A merge-audit script (phase 0) prints three lists after each merge: the files
  present in either parent and missing from the result (`git diff --diff-filter=D --name-only`
  against each parent), which may hold only deletions someone made on purpose; every Spanish
  literal `main` removed or changed that now exists in `es/`; and the tests `main` added or changed
  that query literal text. The first list exists because a merge has already deleted files nobody
  meant to delete: `75e9164` restored 19 of them, among them the `docs/research/` files
  `CLAUDE.md` cites.
- **Conflicts.** In a source file where `main` edited a literal the branch had already replaced by a
  catalog reference, the branch's reference stays and `main`'s copy change moves into the catalogs:
  `en` first (English is the source, D5), so the lock marks `es` and `de` stale; then `es` takes
  `main`'s new Spanish verbatim; `de` is redone; each is recorded with
  `npm run i18n:lock -- --stamp <key>` (§ 7.3). Keeping the branch side and stopping there drops
  María's copy change and nothing turns red, because the lock only sees English change. Nothing on
  `main` writes `src/i18n/` until the launch, so a conflict inside it means a second writer: the
  resolver stops and finds out why, and never merges `lock.json` by hand. Tests that arrive from
  `main` querying literal Spanish text keep passing while the pin is `es` (§ 1.1 O10);
  they move to catalog references when their area migrates.
- Commits, comments, tests and specs authored in the branch are written in English from the first
  commit (D5). What arrives from `main` stays Spanish until the sweep.
- Dependencies are installed in the worktree with `npm ci`, not linked: a junction to `main`'s
  `node_modules` has already deleted `node_modules/.bin` twice when a worktree was removed.

### 9.3 The repository sweep (last)

The sweep runs on `main` after the launch merge, one pass per PR, each pass in its own commits so a
failed proof reverts alone, each with a short announced freeze of `main` (§ 1.1 O2). A
freeze lasts from cutting a pass to merging it, and the pass's gates bound it: the unit suite takes
about 75 s (`.husky/pre-push`), and the deploy e2e list took 21.1 min at 22 specs
(`scripts/deploy-prod.sh:156`). The alternative keeps the sweep last in the branch, with `main`
frozen from the sweep until the launch merge.

Four passes, each with its own proof. The four gates (`npx tsc -b --noEmit`, `npm run test`,
`npm run lint`, `npm run lint:ui`) run after every pass, not only after the renames.

1. **File names and identifiers** are renamed with the TypeScript language service, imports
   included, run over a program that also includes `tests/`, `scripts/` and `eslint-rules/`. Renames
   go first, and a file move is a `git mv` commit that changes nothing in the moved files except
   import specifiers: git tracks a rename by content similarity, and a file whose comments were just
   rewritten can fall below its threshold. Proof: `tsc -b`, the full suite, e2e and lint, plus a
   string-reference audit, because `tsc -b` covers only `src`, `vite.config.ts` and `bench/`
   (`tsconfig.json` references those three projects; none includes `tests/`, `scripts/` or
   `eslint-rules/`) and many references are strings:
   - `git grep -F <old name>` finds nothing outside the five historical directories.
   - Every path in `scripts/`, `package.json`, `.husky/`, `playwright*.config.ts`, `eslint-rules/`
     and the path tables of `tests/repo/` resolves to a tracked file. The ones that bite: the 27
     spec paths of `scripts/deploy-prod.sh:175-202` (Playwright 1.56.1 printed "1 passed" and exited
     0 when given one existing and one missing path, measured during the review of this spec; the
     repository asks for `^1.58.2`), the rows of `tests/repo/superficies-declaradas.test.ts`,
     `scripts/medir-bordes-lint.mjs:66,73,78` (a Spanish identifier inside regexes, and a path list)
     and the `TECHOS` keys of `eslint-rules/deuda-espacio-de-pieza.js:40`.
   - `deploy-prod.sh --ensayo` passes, and the `test -f` check of phase 0 covers its spec list.
2. **Comments** are translated. Proof: file by file, the AST printed with comments removed
   (`ts.createPrinter({ removeComments: true })`) is identical before and after, and so is the
   multiset of directive comments: `@vitest-environment` (193 files), `eslint-disable` (188 files),
   `@ts-expect-error`, `@ts-ignore`, `// state-encoded ID` (14), `@cascade` (8 files), `shape:`,
   `i18n-ignore`, `ponytail`, and JSDoc tags. The AST proof alone cannot see a translation that
   drops or alters one of them. Translating also moves cited lines (the `PieceRenderer.tsx:209` of
   `CLAUDE.md`): pass 4 re-checks every `file:line` in the living docs it translates. This spec sits
   in a historical directory, so its cites stay as of `b0299b3b`.
3. **Test titles** (`describe`, `it`, `test`) are translated. Proof: only those literals change in
   the AST. An `.each` table keeps its rows and translates its title with the format string
   (`$tool`, `%s`); a template-literal title is translated by hand from a generated list. Every

### 9.4 Who does what

The split is by role. Subagents run on Sonnet by default, to save tokens (Samuel's default); a
phase plan may name a stronger model for a step, with the reason. Subagents do the mechanical work:
string extraction and catalog drafts, and the passes of § 9.3 under their proofs. The gates check
what they write (`tsc -b`, the lock, the lint rule, the AST proofs), which is why the cheaper model
is acceptable. Design, the failing tests the subagents implement against, and the final "done"
judgment stay with the orchestrator, who also merges `main` into the branch (§ 9.1). Subagents
neither run the four gates nor commit; the orchestrator does both.
